You are preparing a photograph to be relit. Your job is to say what is in it, so that a lighting setup written in general terms can be aimed at this particular subject.

Look at the attached picture and write an inventory of the subject's surfaces:

- What the subject is, concretely -- not "a person" but what they are wearing, not "a vehicle" but what kind, what it is made of, its colours, condition and markings.
- Which surfaces face the camera and which turn away from it. Name them: the near cheek and jaw, or the near fender and door; the far shoulder, or the far flank.
- The upper surfaces, the leading edges, the rims and edges that would catch a light skimming past.
- Where a centre line falls -- the division a viewer would read as the middle of the subject, running from its top to its base.
- What separates the subject from the background: how far it stands clear of it, and what the background actually is.

Describe only what is there. Do not invent a surface the picture does not show, and do not describe the light that is on it now -- the light is about to be replaced, and naming it is how it survives into the new picture.

Write one paragraph of plain prose in the present tense. No preamble, no title, no list, no commentary. Output only the paragraph.

Two broad soft sources stand close in on either side of the subject, one gelled cool and one gelled warm, and the subject sits nearer to them than to anything behind it, so it takes their colour more strongly than the room does. The cool source washes right across the surfaces that turn away from the camera on its side, tinting them plainly and unmistakably. The warm one lies across the surfaces that face the camera on the other side, and across anything the subject is wearing or carrying, tinting them just as plainly. The two meet along the subject's centre line and mix there without either winning, so almost no part of the subject keeps its original neutral colour. Behind the subject the room turns a corner: two plain walls meeting in a vertical edge that falls just behind the subject's highest point, one wall angled toward each source. Each wall is lit only by the source on its side and takes that gel flatly and evenly across its whole face, so the corner reads in the finished picture as two fields of colour divided by one hard line -- a consequence of where the walls and the lights are, never a painted backdrop. The light is soft and even, with no hard shadow edge anywhere, gentle falloff, the whole picture bright and low in contrast, the subject luminous rather than modelled. The cool source is gelled a desaturated sea green; the warm source a soft coral red.

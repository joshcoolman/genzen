Two broad soft sources stand close in on either side of the subject, one cool and one warm, and they light the subject itself far more than anything else. The cool one washes right across the surfaces that turn away from the camera on its side, tinting them plainly and unmistakably. The warm one lies across the surfaces that face the camera on the other side, and across anything the subject is wearing or carrying, tinting them just as plainly. The two meet along the subject's centre line and mix there without either winning, so almost no part of the subject keeps its original neutral colour. Behind the subject the same two colours continue onto the background as two flat fields divided by one hard vertical edge that falls just behind the subject's highest point. The light is soft and even, with no hard shadow edge anywhere, gentle falloff, the whole picture bright and low in contrast, the subject luminous rather than modelled. The cool source and its field are a desaturated sea green; the warm source and its field are a soft coral red.

You are writing the instruction that will relight one photograph.

You are given two things: an inventory of the subject's surfaces in this picture, and a lighting setup written in general terms. The setup was written to work on any subject, so it refers to surfaces by their role -- the side that turns away, the surfaces that face the camera, the upper edge, the centre line. Your job is to bind every one of those references to a surface the inventory actually names.

Rules, in the order they matter:

- **The subject takes the light before the background does.** Say which of this subject's named surfaces each source falls on, plainly and unmistakably, and say that those surfaces take the colour. A relight that changes only the background is the failure this pass exists to prevent, and it is the one that happens when the subject's surfaces go unnamed.
- **Write the subject's half first**, then the background's. That order is load-bearing: leading with the background produces a backdrop swap with the subject left untouched.
- **Keep every quantity the setup states.** Its colours, its contrast, how hard or soft the shadows are, how bright the picture is, where an edge falls -- these are the setup, not suggestions. Change none of them.
- **Change nothing about the subject itself.** Not the pose, not the framing, not what it is or what it is made of. You are aiming lights, not redressing the picture.
- **Drop what does not apply.** Where the setup names a surface this subject does not have, use the nearest surface the inventory does name, or leave that clause out. Never invent an anatomy to satisfy the wording.

You are given the picture as well so the placement can be specific to what is actually there.

Write one paragraph of plain prose in the present tense, describing the finished photograph at rest. No preamble, no title, no list, no commentary. Output only the paragraph.

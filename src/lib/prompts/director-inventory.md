You are looking at stills taken from the clips of one short film, in order. Your
job is to name the primary elements they have in common, so that a reference
sheet can be drawn for each one.

You will be told which kind to inventory. Return only that kind.

**Characters** are the elements that have to stay consistent from scene to
scene. People, and key objects treated the same way: a car two people ride in
is a character, because the next scene has to show the same car. A passer-by,
a background extra, a prop nobody handles twice is not.

**Locations** are where the action happens. One per distinct place: if every
clip is the same warehouse, that is one location, however the camera moves
inside it. An indoor scene and an outdoor scene are two.

Be conservative. A short film has two or three characters and one or two
locations; five of either means you have counted incidental things. If the
stills genuinely show only one, return one.

For each element:

- **name** -- two or three words, what a person would call it out loud. "The
  woman in the red coat", "the black sedan", "the loading dock". Not a label
  like "Character 1".
- **description** -- what a picture of this element has to get right, written
  from the stills and nothing else. For a character: build, age, face, hair,
  clothing, and anything they carry. For a location: the space, its surfaces,
  its light, what is in it. Concrete and visual. Do not invent a backstory, a
  name, or a detail no still shows -- if the face is never clear, say what can
  be seen and stop there.
- **frames** -- the numbers of the two or three stills that show this element
  best, best first. A still where the element is large, sharp and unobstructed
  beats one where it is distant or cut off. Never more than three.

Every frame number you return must be one you were shown.
